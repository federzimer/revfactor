## The fast-time-to-value story

This is the case study that answers the question RevFactor gets most on intake calls: _how fast does this actually work?_

The property onboarded **February 9, 2026.** By the data pull on April 30, **2.8 months** of tenure later, summer pacing had moved from $21,501 same-time-last-year to **$68,960 on-the-books — a +$47,459 swing.** July alone is pacing $34,685 with a 1.70× Market Penetration Index against a 45.7% market occupancy.

That's not eight months of optimization. That's eight weeks.

## What the headline number obscures

The +220.7% pacing lift looks dramatic in part because the prior year's same-period booking curve was unusually thin ($21,501 STLY). The cleaner read is **vs. final 2025 revenue** for the same window: $70,780 final last year, $68,960 already booked this year — pacing **2.6% under final LY with peak July still booking.** July finished 2025 at a strong number; July 2026 is already at $34,685 OTB with a 77% occupancy committed.

In other words: this property had a strong 2025. It is on track to match or exceed it, with two-thirds of the peak month already locked in at a higher rate.

## The Smoky Mountains market context

Gatlinburg sits inside RevFactor's largest concentration of properties — **31 listings across the Smoky Mountains region.** That density gives the team an unusually clean comp-set read: when this property's pacing curve diverges from the market, it's visible immediately, and the playbook for what's working in the market in real time is informed by 30 sister properties.

The market is also unforgiving. Average daily rates in Gatlinburg sit at roughly $1,000–$1,400 for premium cabins. A wrong rate decision on a peak weekend costs four figures, not three.

## What changed in the first 60 days — Fede's read

The first half of this is verified — it appears in the [pillar revenue management guide](/blog/revenue-management-for-short-term-rentals): *"A 6-bedroom in Gatlinburg onboarded in early February 2026 — less than three months before summer. Gatlinburg is event-heavy and pacing-sensitive: by April, summer-2026 on-the-books revenue was $68,960 against $21,501 at the same point last year. That's $47,000 of additional summer pacing read by the forecasting layer in under 90 days, not by changing the property and not by chasing rates the week of the event. The market price index hit 1.33x — the property was outpacing the Gatlinburg market by a third. New owner, same Smoky Mountain peak weeks, different forecasting cadence."*

The deeper read of what specifically changed — Fede's draft for this case study, pending verification:

> *Smoky Mountains is the market we know best. Thirty-one properties in the region means I don't have to guess what's working in early February — I'm seeing it in real time across thirty other calendars. When Rabbit Run came on, the immediate reads were straightforward: the rate floor was too low for a 6BR with this amenity package, the minimum-stay rules were over-restricting July weekend demand, and the booking-curve discipline was reactive instead of pacing-led.*
>
> *The 60-day work was three things, in order. First, re-baseline ADR — $1,041 final 2024 to $1,265 STLY to $1,352 OTB is a comp-set move, not a single price increase. Second, reset minimum-stays month-by-month: tighter in shoulder, looser into peak July where the market over-restricts. Third, pace the calendar. July at 77% on-the-books eight weeks out isn't an algorithm output — it's a strategist watching the curve weekly and holding rate when the rest of the comp set started discounting.*
>
> *The +$47,000 isn't a magic number. It's eight weeks of doing the work the comp-set wasn't.*
