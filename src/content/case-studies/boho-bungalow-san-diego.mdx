## The premium-rate story

Federico Zimerman has discussed this exact pattern in podcast interviews: a portfolio property running **40% occupancy at 3× the revenue of comparable listings** — the math of **"premium pricing for the right guests rather than racing to fill every night."**

This Boho Bungalow case study is that pattern, in numbers: the property is pacing at **29.4% on-the-books occupancy** for summer — modest by absolute standards — and at **$1,143 ADR** with revenue +98.6% ahead of STLY. The competing playbook would have been to drop the rate, fill the calendar, and report higher occupancy with lower per-night yield. RevFactor went the other way.

The result is the **largest ADR climb in the Summer 2026 set:** $717 final 2025 → $818 STLY → $1,143 OTB — a **+$325 jump (+39.7%) in a single year.**

## Why this is hard

San Diego is a high-quality, design-forward STR market. The temptation for owners is two-fold: chase volume because there's enough demand to support it, or chase rate because the leisure premium is real. The wrong move is to do _both_ casually. RevFactor's playbook commits to one — and the calendar follows.

The clearest signal that this is rate-led, not occupancy-led, is **August's 2.15× Market Penetration Index.** When market occupancy collapses to 12% in late summer (as it does here in San Diego's softer back-end), this property is still pulling 25.8% — twice the market's share. That's a property that **doesn't follow the market down.**

## What changed

The visible pattern in the calendar, without disclosing owner-specific configuration:

1. **Rate moved up first; occupancy followed.** STLY ADR was $818; OTB ADR is $1,143. That's a deliberate floor-raising decision made before occupancy was visible. The property's gradual occupancy lift (20.7% STLY → 29.4% OTB) confirms the rate held.
2. **Length-of-stay tuning.** July ADR sits at $1,407 — well above the 3-month average. That's the signal of LOS-discounted bookings clustering in the highest-demand month, where the discount feels like a deal but the per-night math is a win.
3. **Channel + minimum-stay positioning.** A 12% market occupancy in August would suggest the comp-set is over-restricting on minimums. A 2.15× MPI suggests this listing isn't.
