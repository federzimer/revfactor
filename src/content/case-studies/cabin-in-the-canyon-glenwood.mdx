## The single-booking story

Most case studies measure pacing, ADR, and MPI in aggregate. This one is about a single line item in the calendar.

On its **most recent launch property** — Cabin in the Canyon, a 2-bedroom in Glenwood Springs, Colorado — RevFactor secured a **$30,000 booking for the month of June** before the property had accumulated review history or established booking-curve data. That single booking covers more than five years of RevFactor's $320/month management fee on that property.

The aggregate numbers tell the same story:

- **Q1 2026:** $36,273 (vs. $28,423 in Q1 2025) — **+27.6% lift, MPI 1.68×**
- **Summer 2026 pacing:** $29,327 OTB vs. $8,692 same-time-last-year — **+237.4%**
- Premium Colorado market, first-year-on-the-program performance

## Why this matters

A new listing's first 30–60 days are typically the worst time to chase rate. Federico Zimerman has [documented the standard playbook](/blog/revenue-management-for-short-term-rentals): launch 15–25% below market, optimize for occupancy and review velocity, then transition to revenue mode once the algorithm trusts the listing.

The exception is when a high-LOS, high-rate booking is sitting in the calendar that the algorithm wouldn't push for on its own. **Month-long premium bookings are the kind of inventory that algorithms underprice and humans catch.** The math is straightforward: a 30-night booking at $1,000/night closes at $30,000. Even at a 15% LOS discount that's $25,500 on a single guest — more than most launch-window properties earn in their entire first quarter.

The RevFactor playbook here was: hold the rate floor for the LOS-friendly window even as the property launched, lean into the booking when it surfaced, and capture the 5-year-payback inventory before the algorithm filled the calendar with shorter stays at lower per-night rates.

## What changed (Fede's take — pending verification)

> *On a launch property in a premium mountain market, the temptation is always to drop the rate, fill the calendar, get reviews, and then transition. That works for most properties. It doesn't work for a property where the upside is concentrated in a few high-LOS bookings each year. We held a calendar position that the algorithm wouldn't have on its own. The booking that came in covered five years of our fee. That's not a clever trick — it's just the difference between letting the tool run on autopilot and having someone read the calendar week-over-week.*

## The Glenwood Springs context

Glenwood Springs is part of RevFactor's **5-property Colorado / Glenwood concentration.** The market combines premium leisure demand (the hot springs, the resort traffic) with serious seasonality — peak weeks compress, shoulder weeks need defensive pricing. A 1.68× MPI in Q1 means this property is capturing roughly two-thirds more share than its position would predict in the broader Colorado market.

## What it costs

RevFactor manages this property at a flat **$320/month per property.** As Zoey put it, the single $30,000 booking covered five years of fees in one transaction. The +$7,850 Q1 lift covers another **~24 months** on top of that.
