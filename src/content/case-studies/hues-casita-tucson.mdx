## What the numbers say

Q1 2026 finished at **$10,222** for this Tucson studio. The same studio booked **$1,668** in Q1 2025. That's a **+512.7% year-over-year lift** — the largest Q1 swing in the entire RevFactor portfolio for the quarter. The Market Penetration Index sits at **1.15×**, which means the property is also leading its Tucson submarket on a share-of-market basis, not just lifting in isolation.

The cleaner read of this number is: a property that wasn't really moving in 2025 became a top-quartile performer the moment a real revenue strategy went on top of it.

## The portfolio context

Hues Casita is one of three RevFactor-managed properties under [Erin Warren and Kassidy](https://kassidywarren.com/). The other two:

- **Desert Hues** — a 3BR Tucson B&B, Q1 2026 revenue $29,874 vs. $7,753 in Q1 2025 (+285.3%)
- **Little Stuga** — a 1BR cabin on Orcas Island, Washington — booked summer 2026 pacing $19,137 vs. $492 same-time-last-year (the largest summer-pacing read in the portfolio sheet on a relative basis)

Three different markets. Three different property types. One strategist watching all three calendars. As Federico Zimerman put it in the [pillar revenue management guide](/blog/revenue-management-for-short-term-rentals): *"None of the three is a prestige listing. Each runs a different market with different seasonality... three properties, three different markets, one strategist watching all three calendars at once."*

That's the portfolio effect — each property gets the same individual attention, but the lifts compound because the strategist's read on one property informs every other decision in the cluster.

## What changed (Fede's take — pending verification)

> *On a property like Hues Casita, the previous-year number isn't really a baseline. It's a not-yet-trying number. A studio in Tucson with the right design, the right walking distance to U of A, and the right photos has demand the prior pricing wasn't capturing. The first quarter of work isn't dramatic — it's restoring the rate floor, tightening minimum stays for the campus calendar (game weekends, parents weekends, finals week), and watching how the comp set responds. Tucson is a soft-shoulder, hot-peak market. The whole Q1 lift is a peak-week capture story plus a market-rate-floor reset. Nothing fancy.*
