## Who Is This List For?

A host in the US with one to five short-term rentals who keeps cleaning, guests and the listing, and wants someone else to own the revenue side: the nightly rate, minimum stays, gap nights, listing conversion, channel mix and the monthly read on how it is going. That is a different purchase from pricing software, which sets a number from the rules you give it, and from full-service property management, which takes operations too. Both are covered elsewhere on this site: [the best STR revenue management companies and tools](/blog/best-str-revenue-management-companies-2026/) compares managed services against software across all portfolio sizes, and the [beginner's guide to dynamic pricing](/blog/dynamic-pricing-str-beginners-guide/) explains what the tools do on their own.

## How Did We Score the Services?

To be on the list, a company's own website has to offer done-for-you pricing to an owner of one listing in the US. Freelance directory profiles are excluded. Each service is then scored at its cheapest published tier that includes daily or near-daily rate management, minimum-stay and length-of-stay rules, listing conversion work, a recurring strategy touchpoint with a named person and a monthly report. Where a site does not evidence one of those, the service stays on the list and loses points on the matching criterion.

Seven criteria, weighted before scoring:

- **Strategy scope beyond the rate (25):** minimum-stay and length-of-stay rules, comp tracking, listing conversion, channel mix, event and season playbooks, launch pricing, calendar work. Each item counts only if the vendor's site describes it.
- **Strategist access (15):** a named strategist or revenue manager, a scheduled recurring session with the owner, and a stated between-session channel with a response commitment, five points each.
- **Fit for one to five US properties (15):** an explicit yes at one property, US-dollar pricing with US-hours support, and a service not positioned mainly at ten-plus units or property managers, five points each.
- **Fee transparency (10):** one unambiguous published price for the qualifying tier at one listing, with every fee on the pricing surface.
- **Cost at one property, year one (10):** under $2,000 scores 10, $2,000 to $2,999 scores 8, $3,000 to $3,999 scores 6, $4,000 to $4,999 scores 5, $5,000 and up scores 2, not published scores 3.
- **Owner control and terms (10):** works inside your own accounts, publishes its term and cancellation, no lock-in or exit fee.
- **Evidence (15):** portfolio size disclosed, a headline result with its comparison basis stated, case studies with numbers, third-party reviews.

Sources are each vendor's own homepage, pricing page, FAQ, service and case-study pages, captured on September 16, 2026, plus the PriceLabs RM Partners Directory for review counts. Where a vendor does not publish something, it scores lower on that criterion rather than getting the benefit of the doubt. Ties would be broken by strategy scope; none occurred.

## Who Is Not on This List, and Why?

## How Do You Choose Between Them?

**What do you want handed off?** If it is the nightly rate and the rules around it, Revenuenaire Core at $225 or Hostlyft at $140 is the published floor. If it is the rate plus minimum stays, listing conversion, channel mix and a person who calls you every month, compare RevFactor and Revenuenaire Pro, which land within $400 of each other over a year.

**Who will you talk to, and when?** Ask for the name of the person on your calendar, how often you meet and how you reach them between meetings. Three of the seven publish an answer to all three. The signs that it is time to hire at all are covered in [when to hire an Airbnb revenue management company](/blog/airbnb-revenue-management-company/).

**What does leaving cost?** Month to month with your own accounts means you lose nothing but the strategist. A setup fee, a minimum term or a notice period means you are buying a relationship you should test on the first call. Two services on this list publish an exit cost, one does not publish its terms at all, and that one is ours.
