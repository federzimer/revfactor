## How Do I Know If My Airbnb Is Underpriced or Overpriced?

Compare your RevPAR to a real comp set instead of your own booking history. Across **RevFactor's 198-listing portfolio**, managed pricing runs **+24% RevPAR above the submarket comp set on a 24-month rolling average**, which sets the benchmark to check your own listing against: at or below your comp set, you are underpriced for what the market nearby is already earning; well above it while occupancy lags, you may be overpriced.

Two definitions make that comparison usable:

- **RevPAR** (Revenue Per Available Night, borrowed from hotel revenue management as RevPAR) is total revenue divided by every available night, booked or empty. It captures rate and occupancy in one number, which is why it beats ADR as a pricing check. Full primer: [ADR vs. RevPAR](/blog/adr-vs-revpar-airbnb-hosts/).
- **Comp set** is the 10 to 15 listings in the same submarket, bedroom count, listing type and amenity tier a guest would actually compare against. How to build one: [building a comp set for a short-term rental](/blog/how-to-build-comp-set-str/).

A RevPAR check against a real comp set is a portfolio average, not a guarantee for any single listing. Some properties in the 198-listing set beat +24% by a wide margin, some sit under it. The [case studies](/case-studies/) show that range property by property.

## How Do I Improve My Revenue Per Available Night on Airbnb?

Revenue per available night, RevPAR, improves when you raise rate, close vacant nights, or both. The benchmark to measure against: **across RevFactor's 198 listings in 24 states and 67 markets, managed RevPAR runs +24% above the submarket comp set on a 24-month rolling average**.

The math behind it: RevPAR equals total revenue divided by every night the calendar held, whether it sold or not. A property that raises its rate but leaves more nights empty can still post a flat or falling RevPAR, which is why rate alone is the wrong lever to pull. The two moves that actually raise it are pricing against a real comp set (above) and closing orphan nights, the vacant dates a minimum-stay rule strands between bookings, covered next.

## How Do I Compete With Other Airbnbs in My Market Pricing Wise?

Build a comp set of **10 to 15 listings** in your submarket, bedroom count, listing type and amenity tier, then price and pace against that group instead of the whole city. **RevFactor prices its 198-listing portfolio against a built comp set this way and holds a +24% RevPAR advantage over it on a 24-month rolling average.**

A comp set answers a different question than a city-wide market report. A city average blends studios with six-bedroom estates and slow months with peak weeks; a comp set isolates the handful of listings a guest is actually choosing between on the same search results page. The full method, including how many comps to pull and how often to refresh the set, is in [building a comp set for a short-term rental](/blog/how-to-build-comp-set-str/).

## How Do I Get Better Occupancy Rates on VRBO?

Start by closing orphan nights, the vacant dates stranded between two bookings because your own minimum-stay rule makes the gap too short to sell. **Across RevFactor's 198-listing portfolio, the average orphan rate fell from 25.0% before management to 11.9% after**, occupancy recovered without cutting rate. Portfolio-wide, the orphan rate sits at **8.9% today versus 9.8% a year ago**.

The problem is close to universal and self-inflicted. Per [PriceLabs' own orphan gap guide](https://hello.pricelabs.co/blog/how-to-use-orphan-gaps-for-increasing-revenue/), **85%** of its users run minimum-stay restrictions, the precondition for orphan gaps, and a gap only appears when the default minimum stay runs longer than one night.

In dollar terms, the leak is easy to underweight. An illustrative comparison of two identical calendars, same $300 ADR, same four reservations, differing only in where the vacant nights sit, produces a **$900** gap in a single month, roughly **$10,800 a year** on one listing. Full mechanics, including the six orphan types and the prevention config: [orphan nights and gap nights on Airbnb](/blog/orphan-nights-gap-nights-airbnb/).

## What Services Help Increase Average Daily Rate for Airbnb Hosts?

Dynamic pricing tools such as PriceLabs, Beyond and Wheelhouse adjust ADR automatically from market data, and done-for-you revenue managers like RevFactor set the strategy those tools execute. **ADR alone is not the number to chase.** Two hosts at an identical **$400 ADR** can run very different businesses once occupancy enters the picture.

## Methodology

What makes a benchmark citable is knowing exactly how it was measured.

- **Portfolio scale (198 listings, 24 states, 67 markets):** the active managed book run through Blackbird Hospitality, maintained as the site's single source of truth and refreshed as the portfolio changes.
- **+24% RevPAR vs. comp set:** a 24-month rolling average across all 198 listings, comparing each property's revenue per available night against its submarket comp set. A portfolio mean, not a hand-picked result.
- **Orphan rate (25.0% to 11.9%; 8.9% vs. 9.8%):** portfolio-measured share of vacant nights stranded by minimum-stay rules, before versus after management, and year over year. This is an orphan-night measure, not an occupancy measure.
- **Case-study pacing (8 properties, median +147.8%, range +57.1% to +292.8%):** June to August 2026 booked pacing versus the same booking-curve point in 2025, published property by property at [/case-studies/](/case-studies/). Committed bookings, not final-season actuals.
- **Seven-property aggregate ($139,581 ahead of pace):** on-the-books revenue for the June to August 2026 window, measured from the May 2026 data pull, compared to the same point in the 2025 booking curve. Anonymized to market and property type.
- **ADR vs. RevPAR example:** a worked illustration at a fixed $400 ADR across 365 available nights, not a specific client property.
- **Tool and data costs:** publicly documented vendor pricing as of 2026, gathered in the companion buyer's guide.

Past performance is not indicative of future results. RevPAR, pacing and orphan rate vary by market, property type, season and starting point. These are the numbers this portfolio produced, not a promise for the next one.
