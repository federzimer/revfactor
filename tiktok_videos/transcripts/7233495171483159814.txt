Ever wondered why some businesses thrive, while others don't? Let me explain the concept of survivorship bias, using a fascinating WW2 example, and how it applies to businesses today. Back in W2, the US military wanted to reinforce planes, returning from battle. They studied planes that returned safely, noticing bullet holes in wings, tails, and fuselages. So, they wanted to add armor there. But statistician Abraham Wald, realized they were only analyzing surviving planes. The ones that didn't make it back could have had bullet holes in more critical areas, like engines. He proposed reinforcing those areas instead, and it worked. Survivorship bias is when we focus on success stories, while overlooking failures. In business, we often hear about wildly successful companies, like Apple or Tesla. But we don't hear about the countless startups that failed. What can you do? One, analyze both success and failure stories. Two, challenge conventional wisdom and ask what's missing. Three, be willing to adapt and improve.
