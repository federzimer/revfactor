## The script

### 0:00 to 0:15 · Cold open (hook)
*(Fede on camera, direct address. No intro card first. Open on the strongest line.)*

> "A plane seat is worthless the second the plane takes off. Your Fourth of July is worthless too, if it sits unbooked. Last summer we added a hundred and thirty-nine thousand dollars across seven Airbnbs, on the books, ahead of the year before. Same houses, same owners, no new furniture. Here is exactly what we changed."

**On screen:** "+$139,580 · 7 properties · one summer" (JetBrains Mono, cedar on bone). Hold 2 seconds.

### 0:15 to 0:45 · The promise
> "I spent ten years in the airline industry before I ever touched a short-term rental. Airlines have this figured out. They sell the same seat at forty different prices and they never leave money on the table. Most hosts do the opposite. They set one price, maybe turn on a pricing tool, and hope. I am going to walk you through the three things we actually did on these seven properties. You can copy all of it."

**On screen chapters preview:** "1. Minimum-stay · 2. The two-cycle climb · 3. Pacing." 

### 0:45 to 1:30 · The setup
> "These seven are spread across the country. A cabin in the Smokies, a lake house on Lake Michigan, a coastal place in Myrtle Beach, a few others. Different markets, different guests. What they had in common: every one of them was underpriced on the days that mattered and overpriced on the days nobody was booking anyway. That is the pattern we fix."

**B-roll:** intercut anonymized calendar/dashboard screens (blur any identifying names). Pull from the brain comp-set and calendar clips.

### 1:30 to 4:00 · Part 1: Minimum-stay flexibility (beat the market floor)
> "Here is the first mistake. A host sets a three-night minimum for the whole summer and leaves it there. Feels safe. It is not. Every orphan night, every single gap between bookings, becomes unsellable. That is a seat taking off empty. What we do is flex the minimum. Longer minimums when demand is high and you want full weeks. Shorter minimums the moment a gap appears, so we can actually sell it. On the cabin, opening up those gaps alone was a big chunk of the lift."

**On screen:** simple before/after calendar graphic. "Rigid 3-night min → flexible by demand." Show one anonymized gap getting filled.
**Source clips:** the minimum-stay reel and the orphan-gap / cancellation-policy clip from the brain.

### 4:00 to 6:30 · Part 2: The two-cycle ADR climb
> "Second thing. You do not jump your nightly rate straight to the top and wait. That kills your booking pace and the algorithm buries you. We climb it in two cycles. Start a notch under the market so you get early bookings and momentum, let the calendar fill, and then raise the rate into the demand you just created. You are not guessing at a price. You are letting the market tell you how high it will go, then going there. On the lake house that two-step climb is most of the plus twenty-three thousand."

**On screen:** a line graph, ADR climbing in two steps as occupancy fills. Label "Cycle 1: build pace" and "Cycle 2: raise into demand."
**Source clips:** the pricing/ADR reels and the "price like a professional revenue manager" momentum clip.

### 6:30 to 8:00 · Part 3: Pacing discipline
> "Third. Most hosts look at last year's number and copy it. That is a photograph. You need the movie. Every week we look at how fast each property is booking compared to the same week last year, and we adjust. Booking faster than last year? The price goes up, because demand is higher than we thought. Booking slower? We do not panic-drop. We adjust the minimum first and protect the rate. That weekly discipline is the difference between guessing and managing."

**On screen:** "The picture vs the movie." Show a pacing curve, this year vs last year.
**Source clips:** the pacing/momentum clip and any "revenue manager mindset" clip.

### 8:00 to 9:30 · The results
> "So here is what those three moves did across the seven properties, on the books, ahead of last year."

**On screen:** build the data table row by row, animate to the total. Land on "+$139,580."

> "The cabin, up forty-seven thousand. The lake house, twenty-three. And it keeps going. Seven properties, a hundred and thirty-nine thousand dollars more than the year before. And the calendars were still booking when we pulled these numbers. Nothing here required new furniture or a new listing. It is the same houses, priced like an airline instead of a guess."
