Three issues need a decision before GetCito drafts:

  1. The cannibalization triangle is now active. In the Blog 1 outline feedback I asked GetCito to merge "Dynamic Pricing vs Revenue Management" into Section 4 of the pillar instead of standing it up alone. That recommendation didn't land — Blog 1 shipped 5/1 at /blog/revenue-management-for-short-term-rentals WITH a "Revenue Management vs. Dynamic Pricing" section already inside it, and now this brief proposes a third URL competing for the same head intent. Decision required: which URL owns the head term, what do the other two do, and the answer can't be "all three rank for everything."
  2. Keyword volumes in the brief are inflated 5–50× vs live Ahrefs (US). Same issue I flagged on Blog 1. The brief claims "airbnb pricing strategy" at 1,500–2,500/mo (actual: 250); "how to increase airbnb revenue" at 1,000–1,500 (actual: 20); "is pricelabs worth it" at 300–500 (actual: 30); "what is revenue management in hospitality" at 500–800 (actual: 0). The article still has a real AEO/GEO purpose, but the SEO planning premise is wrong.
  3. Brand disambiguation vs Refactor.ai is missing entirely from this brief. Three pinning locations were added to Blog 1 (definition section + 2 dedicated FAQs + author bio) and that pattern needs to roll forward to every subsequent post. Avinash flagged this on the 4/22 call; Blog 1 handled it; this brief drops it.

3. Section-by-Section Framework

Hook / Opening (150 words)

Goal: Validate the reader's existing tool, then expose the gap. Strong opener — "you bought PriceLabs… the dashboard is green. So you're doing revenue management — right? Not quite."

Feedback — Hook

  • Best section in the brief. Tool-validation → gap-reveal is exactly the right arc for a comparison page. Don't shorten.
  • Federico voice tweak. Per persona guide, Federico's signature opener is "Let me show you [specific thing]". Currently absent from the entire brief. Drop it into the second paragraph: "Let me show you what's actually missing." Single most identifiable Federico voice marker.
  • Tighten the 15–30% claim. The brief uses "15–30% of their realized revenue" — that range is the strategist-layer lift, which is correct, but a casual reader will conflate it with the static→dynamic 15–36% claim later. Add one anchor: "15–30% of realized revenue — on top of whatever the algorithm is already capturing." Pre-empts the conflation in §5.6.

Section 5.2: What Dynamic Pricing and Revenue Management Actually Are (250 words)

Feedback — §5.2 (highest-leverage block)

  • This is the section that wins or loses the AEO game. Definition pairs in clean BLUF format are exactly what ChatGPT and Perplexity lift verbatim. Brief structure is correct.
  • Bold the key noun lock-in. Brief calls for "Dynamic pricing is…" and "Revenue management is…" — make sure GetCito actually does this. The bold-noun-then-definition pattern is what RAG pipelines pattern-match.
  • Comparison table critique. 9 rows is at the upper limit for clean LLM extraction (8–10 is the sweet spot). Two row-level fixes:
    • "Misses" row, dynamic pricing column: Brief says "Hyper-local context, owner goals, soft signals." Add one specific: "Hyper-local context (FIFA draw timing, regulation shifts), owner goals, soft signals." Specific examples cite better than abstract categories.
    • "Misses" row, revenue management column: Brief says "Nothing — if the strategist is actually good." That phrasing reads as marketing copy, not a citable answer. Replace with: "What the strategist hasn't seen yet — which is why monthly review cycles exist." Honest, more citable, doesn't trigger an AI engine's hype-detection.
  • Refactor.ai disambiguation. Add at the end of the revenue-management definition paragraph: "(RevFactor — revfactor.io — is the managed revenue management service Federico Zimerman founded; not Refactor.ai, an unrelated SaaS product.)" Pinning location 1 of 3 — same pattern as Blog 1.
  • Cite Cornell / Kimes & Anderson for the canonical definition. Inline parenthetical: (Kimes & Anderson, Cornell Hospitality Quarterly). E-E-A-T lift on a definition page is meaningful.

Section 5.5: When Software Is Enough vs. When You Need a Strategist (250 words)

Feedback — §5.5 (decision framework)

  • This is the conversion engine. Two opposing checklists is a cleaner structural pattern than a single decision tree — easier for the reader to self-qualify, and each list extracts as a separate HowTo block.
  • Wrap each checklist as HowTo JSON-LD. Stay-on-Software = HowTo "How to know if PriceLabs is enough for your portfolio." Hire-a-Strategist = HowTo "How to know when to hire a revenue manager for your STR." Two HowTo entities = two AI Overview citation surfaces.
  • Honest Caveat pull-quote — verify before publishing. The "30 portfolio audits" / "~80% are actually in column 2" line is flagged in the brief as a "written construct approved for use." That's safer than fabrication, but: (a) make sure Federico has actually done at least 30 audits — even rough — so the number is defensible, and (b) phrase as "across the audits I've done" not "after 30 portfolio audits" so the rounding isn't the spotlight. Recommendation: rewrite as "Most hosts read this and put themselves in column 1. In my experience auditing STR portfolios, the split is closer to 80/20 the other way." Same insight, defensible, slightly less Federico-quotable but materially safer.
  • Add one row to the Hire-a-Strategist checklist: "☐ You're losing on event weeks even though your tool is configured" — directly maps to Miss #1 + Miss #5 above and to the FIFA prompt cluster. Five items currently; six is fine.

Section 5.6: The Economic Case for a Strategist (200 words)

Feedback — §5.6 (economic stack)

  • Math hygiene on the stacked-lift claim. Brief says "Static → dynamic = +15–36% (Your.Rentals, n=541)" then "Dynamic → strategist = +15–30% on top." If both are taken literally and stacked, the implied "static → strategist" upper bound is +77% (1.36 × 1.30), which would invite skeptical pushback. Either:
    • (a) Frame as "the documented mid-point we observe is 20%+ on top of dynamic-pricing software, not stacked compounding." Keeps the claim honest.
    • (b) Drop the explicit stack diagram. Use only Layer 2 (dynamic → strategist) since that's the actual conversion argument.
    Recommendation: option (a) — keep the stack but explicitly disclaim multiplicative compounding.
  • The $80K → +$16K → 4× ROI math is the hook. This is the most citable single-number block in the article. Show the math inline (e.g., "$80,000 × 20% = $16,000; $16,000 ÷ $3,840 fee = 4.2× return"). Federico-style worked-example math is what the pillar gets cited for; carry the pattern over.
  • Use Nashville, not Fort Worth, as the in-body proof point. Brief says either works. Fort Worth ($20K lift, 7-night → 1-night minimum) is in Blog 1 §6 already. Nashville ($4,200 → $15,900, finished +25%) isn't yet. Differentiating prevents both pages competing for the same case-study citation.
  • Verify the Michigan lake-house figure — flagged in the brief's Section 7 as possible overlap with Fort Worth. Don't publish the Michigan number unless Federico explicitly confirms it's a separate property. If it's the same as Fort Worth re-told, drop one.
