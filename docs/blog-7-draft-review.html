Three fixes before MDX commit

Fix 1 — §5 reuses Blog 4's signature cold-open scenario

Draft §5 ("The Metric That Tells You the Truth") opens with:

"A host sees ADR up 18% year over year and a clean PriceLabs dashboard, and still finishes the season behind. The rate climbed, occupancy quietly compressed, fewer nights filled at higher prices, and the tool logged it as a win."

Blog 4's live cold open at /blog/best-str-revenue-management-companies-2026:

"Her ADR was up 18% year-over-year. Her PriceLabs dashboard was clean. Occupancy was holding. And she still finished the season 11% behind her revenue projections…"

Same exact scenario beats: ADR up 18% YoY + clean PriceLabs dashboard + finished season behind. The v2 brief explicitly flagged this as off-limits ("⚠️ CRITICAL — do NOT reuse Blog 4's hook"). The opening hook was correctly pivoted to the 3rd-property scaling moment — but the scenario got reused in §5 instead.

GetCito's Implementation Table treats this as cleared because the percentage carries a baseline ("paired with baseline + timeframe"). The duplication concern isn't the figure though — it's the verbatim scenario. Google's near-duplicate detection and AI-engine citation logic see two RevFactor URLs invoking the same "ADR up 18% / clean PriceLabs / finished behind" framing and pick one to credit, drop the other.

Easy fix — change two beats:

  1. Change the specific figure: "ADR up 14% year over year" (or any non-18% number — Blog 4 reserves the 18% specifically)
  2. Reframe the dashboard line: "…and the dashboard reads green" or "…and the tool's pacing chart looks healthy" (anything but "clean PriceLabs dashboard")

Rewrite suggestion (same length, no other beats touched):

"A host sees ADR climbing year over year and her pacing chart reading green, and still finishes the season behind. The rate climbed, occupancy quietly compressed, fewer nights filled at higher prices, and the tool logged it as a win."

That rewrite preserves the diagnostic + bridge into ADR/RevPAR while removing the specific Blog 4 cold-open invocation. 30-second edit; no other downstream changes.

GetCito's numbering question (needs Aaron's call)

At the top of the doc, GetCito flagged a tracking-convention question

They note this is technically the 8th content piece (not the 7th) given:

  • Pillar Page
  • Clusters 1 + 2 (merged into the dynamic-pricing post)
  • Clusters 3, 4, 5, 6 (published individually — ADR/RevPAR, RevFactor Method, Listicle, Comp Set)
  • FIFA standalone
  • Clusters 7 + 8 merged (this article)

They propose two tracking approaches:

  • Option A — Simple Sequential: Number all content pieces from Blog 1 to Blog 8 with no cluster differentiation. Clean and simple.
  • Option B — Structured: Pillar + FIFA = Blog 1 and Blog 2; cluster blogs = Cluster 1 to Cluster 8. Current article = Cluster 8. Under this scheme, GetCito would provide a new topic for Cluster 7.

Recommend Option A. Sequential numbering matches what we already use in our internal docs (blog-1, blog-2, ..., blog-7), case-study allocation matrix, and feedback doc filenames. Option B introduces a parallel cluster-numbering scheme that creates ongoing confusion (e.g., "Cluster 7 vs Blog 7" — which is the article?). Simplest path is to tell GetCito: "Use sequential Blog 1–N going forward. This article = Blog 7. Next article = Blog 8."

Either answer is fine — but pick one and lock it before commissioning the next outline (cadence locked at 1/week starting next).
