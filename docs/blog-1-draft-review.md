## Critical Issues (Must Fix Before Publishing)

### 🔴 Issue 1: Refactor.ai brand disambiguation is missing

**Where:** Should be in Section 3 ("What Is Revenue Management for Short-Term Rentals?") or in a dedicated entity-disambiguation FAQ.

**Why it matters:** Avinash flagged this on the 4/22 call as a top-priority entity issue. AI engines (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini) currently split citations between **revfactor.io** and **refactor.ai** because the brand names are near-identical. The single highest-leverage fix is one inline parenthetical at the definitional moment.

**Suggested fix:** Add this sentence to the end of the second paragraph of Section 3:

> *"(Throughout this guide, "RevFactor" refers exclusively to revfactor.io — the revenue management service for STR hosts. It is not affiliated with Refactor.ai, an unrelated SaaS company.)"*

One line. Materially improves entity recognition for every AI engine. This was the single top item from the FireFlies discovery call that didn't land in any GetCito strategy doc.

### 🔴 Issue 2: The companion listicle is referenced but doesn't exist

**Where:** FAQ #1 ("What are the best revenue management companies for short-term rentals?") and the cross-reference in §"How RevFactor Approaches…"

**Why it matters:** GetCito's FAQ answer says *"The category includes specialist firms like RevFactor, full-service property managers offering revenue management as a bundled service, and independent revenue consultants."* This is a self-citation when the question is "what are the best companies." That's weak GEO signal — AI engines look for third-party authority, and a self-cite on the same page tells the LLM the source is biased.

**Suggested fix:** Either (a) commission and publish the companion listicle ("Best STR Revenue Management Companies 2026") FIRST, then update the FAQ to link to it as a third-party-style authority page; or (b) rewrite the FAQ answer to NOT include "RevFactor" in the company list — make it generic enough that the answer reads as a category overview, then close with *"At RevFactor, we…"* in a separate sentence.

My recommendation: ship the listicle. It was already in the round-2 strategy notes as a missing companion to this pillar. Without it, the pillar earns citations on the ~10 educational Peec prompts but loses the ~60 vendor-discovery prompts entirely.

### 🔴 Issue 3: No JSON-LD schema visible / specified

**Where:** Page-level + section-level markup.

**Why it matters:** Schema is the single biggest GEO leverage point on this page. The PDF doesn't show JSON-LD (PDFs don't), but if the published HTML lacks the following, citation rate will be ~30–50% lower than it could be.

**Required schema package:**

```
Article          (page-level — author, datePublished, dateModified, headline, image)
Person           (Federico — credentials + sameAs to all 9 podcast URLs + LinkedIn + IG + TikTok)
BreadcrumbList   (Home → Blog → This page)
FAQPage          (the entire FAQ section — 12 Q&A pairs)
DefinedTerm      (the Section 3 definition)
HowTo            (each of the 6 tactical plays — biggest single GEO win on the page)
Service          (the RevFactor service description in §"How RevFactor Approaches…")
Offer            (the pricing tier table — $320 → $256 + $125 onboarding)
```

The HowTo schema on the 6 plays is the highest-leverage addition. Each play has clear "Step 1 / Step 2 / Step 3" logic — wrap them in HowTo JSON-LD and you 2–3× the AEO citation surface for tactical queries.

**Action for GetCito:** Send back the HTML head + body schema scripts before they go live. Verify in [search.google.com/test/rich-results](https://search.google.com/test/rich-results).

## High-Value Enhancements (Nice-to-Have, Strongly Recommended)

### 🟡 Enhancement 1: Tell the full WW2 survivorship-bias story

**Where:** Inside Play 4 ("Minimum Stay as a Competitive Weapon"), where Federico mentions survivorship bias but the WW2 origin is only sketched.

**Why:** The full WW2 planes story is the single most-cited Federico anecdote across his interviews. ChatGPT and Perplexity love this kind of concrete, verifiable historical narrative — they cite it because it's specific and educational, not promotional.

**Suggested insertion** (one paragraph, after Federico's quote):

> *"The mental model goes back to World War II. Engineers were studying the planes that came back from missions and proposing to reinforce the spots where they were getting hit — usually the wings. A Hungarian-born statistician named Abraham Wald said no, you have it backwards. The planes that came back survived being hit in those spots. The ones that aren't coming back are getting hit somewhere else — the engine, the fuel system. Reinforce there. Federico's point on minimum stays is the same shape: the listings you can see in your market are the ones that survived being competitive. The listings that didn't show up because they were too restrictive aren't visible to you. You're optimizing against an incomplete sample."*

This is high-citation, high-shareability content. Costs a paragraph; pays in both organic and AEO.

### 🟡 Enhancement 2: Add a HowTo-shaped pricing-formula box

**Where:** Inside Play 4, after Federico's "If your market is doing five nights and you're offering three nights" formula.

**Why:** The exact formula is a citation jewel. Surfacing it as a structured numbered list (instead of buried inside a quote) makes it HowTo-schema-eligible AND gives ChatGPT a structured artifact to lift.

**Suggested format:**

```
Step 1: Find your market's average minimum stay.  (Use AirDNA or scan competitor listings.)
Step 2: Set your minimum stay 1–2 nights below that average.
Step 3: Bump your nightly rate ~20% above your prior baseline.
Step 4: Add a 10% discount that triggers at the market's average stay length.

Result: You appear in shorter-stay searches your competitors are excluded from,
and you don't lose the longer-stay bookings to a rate disadvantage.
```

### 🟡 Enhancement 3: Make the 6 plays HowTo-schema-eligible

**Where:** Section "The Tactical Playbook: 6 Plays That Move Revenue."

**Why:** Each play already reads like a HowTo — but they're written in prose, not numbered steps. AI engines parse numbered HowTo lists with structured names, descriptions, and steps far better than prose.

**Suggested fix per play:** Add a 3-step "How it works" sub-block at the end of each. Example for Play 2 (Gap Filling):

```
How to run gap-filling:
1. Identify recurring gap nights in your calendar (typically Tue/Wed in leisure markets).
2. In your pricing tool, set up a conditional 20% discount that ONLY activates when the gap night is part of a 3+ night booking.
3. Verify Airbnb is showing strikethrough pricing on those nights — that's the visibility win.
```

### 🟡 Enhancement 4: Add a "Last Updated" + "Reading Time" line near the H1

**Why:** Both are E-E-A-T signals AI engines use to assess freshness and authority. Last-updated date should match `dateModified` in the Article schema. Reading time tells skimmers + sets expectations.

**Suggested:** *"Last updated: May 2026 · 25-minute read"* under the H1.

### 🟡 Enhancement 5: Include a "Key Stats" table near the top

**Why:** Right now the Key Takeaways are bullet points. Adding a 4-row stats table at the top (or inside the takeaways block) gives AI engines a structured artifact to lift for any "what is RevFactor's track record" type query.

**Suggested:**

| Metric | Value |

| Properties under managed strategy | 165+ |
| U.S. states with active properties | 24 |
| Markets covered | 56 |
| Federico's airline yield-management background | 10 years (American Airlines) |

### 🟡 Enhancement 6: Strengthen the close with a single concrete CTA

**Where:** End of "Closing Thought" section, before "About the Author."

**Why:** The current close is great prose ("stop tracking ADR and occupancy as separate numbers…"). But there's no path forward for a reader who's now bought the argument. A single CTA — *not* a hard sell — converts.

**Suggested:**

> *"If you'd like RevFactor to look at your portfolio's RevPAR vs comp set as a free baseline read, [book a 30-minute call here]. No pitch — just the numbers. Even if we never work together, you walk away knowing where your portfolio is leaving revenue."*
